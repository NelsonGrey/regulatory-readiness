# Regulatory Readiness Engine — Technical Requirements

**Document type:** Technical Requirements Document (TRD)
**Version:** 0.1
**Status:** Proposed / discovery
**Last updated:** August 30, 2026
**Owner:** Mark Nelson

Related documents: [ENGINE_CONCEPT.md](../ENGINE_CONCEPT.md), [Business Requirements](BUSINESS_REQUIREMENTS.md), [Detailed Design](detailed-design/README.md), [AWS Architecture](../ARCHITECTURE_AWS.md).

> This document specifies an evidence-readiness platform: one vertical-neutral engine plus data-defined control packs. It does not specify an automated legal-decision engine, a conformity-assessment service, a public host, or production submission to any authority.

---

## 1. Purpose

The engine must convert fragmented internal and third-party documents into a controlled, source-linked, human-approved readiness record. The central challenge is preserving the distinctions among:

- a regulatory or guidance source;
- a versioned control interpretation (a pack's control snapshot);
- entity classification and applicability;
- an external or internal assertion;
- an automated extraction proposal;
- original evidence and its exact location;
- human approval or rejection;
- current readiness for a named snapshot;
- an export made for a particular downstream purpose.

The platform must remain honest when information is missing, contradictory, stale, conditional, or based on superseded guidance. It must do this for any control pack without changing the domain model.

## 2. Scope and constraints

### 2.1 In scope

- Multi-tenant organizations, workspaces, users, roles, invitations.
- A **control-pack registry**: multiple regulations installed as data in one deployment, isolated from each other.
- Regulated-entity records (product, service, site, or organization) classified per the active pack.
- Versioned regulatory/control snapshots per pack.
- Assignment of controls to internal owners and external parties.
- Requests using expiring, scoped links; inbound email behind a gate.
- Original-document storage, malware scanning, metadata, access classification.
- OCR/structured parsing and source-bound extraction proposals.
- Human claim review, conflict resolution, supersession, approvals.
- Deterministic readiness evaluation and immutable snapshots.
- Portable exports and restricted reviewer sharing.
- Audit, notifications, retention, deletion, observability, support controls.

### 2.2 Out of scope for MVP

- Legal opinions or automated legal applicability decisions.
- Conformity assessment or certification.
- Public publication, data-carrier generation, or resolver hosting (unless a specific pack requires it and passes its gates).
- Production writes to any authority or registry.
- Automatic engineering or sustainability calculations, or test validation.
- Live telemetry and per-instance lifecycle processing.
- Native mobile apps and browser extensions.
- Cross-tenant contributor-data reuse without explicit, granular authorization.

### 2.3 External constraints

- Authority guidance is often non-authoritative and may change.
- Units and formats for some controls remain subject to clarification.
- Registry and access-right behavior may evolve.
- Documents may contain commercially sensitive information.
- Contributors may work in multiple languages and on mobile devices.
- A customer may use a downstream consumer with its own schema and validation rules.

## 3. Architecture principles

1. **Modular monolith first.** One deployable application plus asynchronous workers until scaling or isolation evidence justifies services.
2. **Canonical domain, adapters at boundaries.** Downstream schemas and extraction providers must not define the internal data model.
3. **Packs are data.** A control pack is a versioned data bundle plus declared rules, loaded and validated by the engine — never a code fork. The engine has no `if (pack === …)` branches in domain logic.
4. **Immutable source and history.** Originals, control snapshots, submissions, claim revisions, approvals, and exports are append-oriented.
5. **Human authority over extracted values.** Automation may suggest; only an authorized human can approve.
6. **Tenant isolation by construction.** Every tenant-owned row and object carries a tenant ID; access is enforced in application and database layers.
7. **Pack isolation.** Every pack-owned catalog row carries a pack key; a customer's entity is bound to one pack; evaluations never mix packs.
8. **Least-knowledge contributor links.** A request token exposes only the request and explicitly shared context.
9. **Deterministic readiness.** Readiness is computed from stored rules and states, not generated by an LLM.
10. **Explicit provenance.** Every derived object identifies inputs, actor/process, versions, and time.
11. **Provider portability.** Authentication, object storage, OCR/LLM, email, and export adapters have internal interfaces.
12. **No silent reinterpretation.** New controls create impact records and optional migrations; they never rewrite historical snapshots.

## 7. Control snapshot and applicability model

### 7.1 Pack catalog contents

Each pack's control snapshot MUST preserve, per control: the official identifier and name; the cited regulation article/annex; classification applicability wording; whether the source labels the control mandatory, optional, conditional, duplicate/not-to-fill, or not-yet-required as of a date; and the source disclaimer and retrieval metadata. The engine validates control count, unique keys, referential integrity, rule compilation, known outcomes, and checksum against the pack's `test-vectors.json`.

### 7.2 Applicability output

The engine may return only: `REQUIRED_BY_SNAPSHOT`, `OPTIONAL_IF_AVAILABLE`, `CONDITIONAL_FACT_REQUIRED`, `NOT_YET_REQUIRED_BY_SNAPSHOT`, `DUPLICATE_SOURCE_FIELD`, `NOT_APPLICABLE_TO_CLASSIFICATION`, `NEEDS_SPECIALIST_REVIEW`. It MUST NOT return `LEGALLY_COMPLIANT` or equivalent.

### 7.3 Expression model

Applicability expressions MUST be deterministic and inspectable:

```json
{
  "all": [
    { "fact": "entity.classification", "in": ["<pack-defined value>"] },
    { "snapshot": "<pack-snapshot-key>" }
  ],
  "result": "REQUIRED_BY_SNAPSHOT"
}
```

Unknown facts yield `CONDITIONAL_FACT_REQUIRED`, not a guessed answer.

### 7.4 Snapshot migration

When a new source is approved for a pack: create a new immutable `ControlSnapshot`; produce `ControlChange` rows against the previous snapshot; calculate impacted entities without changing their selected snapshot; show customers additions, removals, applicability changes, name/source changes, and format/unit changes; require an authorized user to adopt the new snapshot per entity; re-evaluate claims without discarding previously approved evidence.

## 8. Workflow state machines

### 8.1 Regulated entity

`DRAFT -> COLLECTING -> REVIEWING -> EVIDENCE_READY`. Alternative: `BLOCKED`, `OUTDATED_SNAPSHOT`, `ARCHIVED`. `EVIDENCE_READY` is derived from a readiness snapshot; not directly editable.

### 8.2 Evidence request

`DRAFT -> SCHEDULED -> SENT -> OPENED -> IN_PROGRESS -> SUBMITTED -> UNDER_REVIEW -> CLOSED`. Alternative terminal: `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED`, `CANCELLED`. Resending MUST NOT create duplicate request items. A reopened request creates a new submission version.

### 8.3 Document

`UPLOADING -> QUARANTINED -> SCANNING -> AVAILABLE -> PARSING -> PARSED`. Alternative: `REJECTED_MALWARE`, `UNSUPPORTED`, `PARSE_FAILED`, `DELETED_PENDING_PURGE`, `PURGED`. No user or worker may download a quarantined or malware-rejected object.

### 8.4 Extraction proposal

`GENERATED -> VALIDATION_WARNING? -> PENDING_REVIEW -> ACCEPTED_AS_CLAIM | REJECTED | SUPERSEDED`. An accepted proposal creates a claim and review decision; the proposal remains immutable.

### 8.5 Claim

`ASSERTED -> PENDING_REVIEW -> APPROVED -> SUPERSEDED`. Alternative: `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN`. Approved claims may become `STALE` through evaluation without rewriting approval history.

### 8.6 Export

`QUEUED -> VALIDATING -> GENERATING -> AVAILABLE -> EXPIRED`. Alternative: `BLOCKED`, `FAILED`, `REVOKED`.

## 10. Document intake and evidence

### 10.1 Supported MVP formats

PDF (incl. scanned); PNG, JPEG, TIFF; XLSX, CSV; DOCX, plain text; EML or provider-normalized inbound email with attachments. Password-protected, signed, macro-enabled, archive, CAD, and proprietary lab files are stored only under explicit supported rules; otherwise `UNSUPPORTED` with manual guidance.

### 10.2 Ingestion controls

Stream upload to quarantine without executing active content; validate media signature not extension; compute SHA-256 before derivatives; run malware and archive-bomb checks; store the original immutable/versioned; extract safe metadata and normalized text in an isolated worker; create derivatives linked to the original hash and tool version; require entity/party associations to be confirmed when not request-derived.

### 10.3 Evidence locations

Locations MUST support: PDF page + bounding rectangle + quoted text; spreadsheet sheet/row/column/cell + displayed/formula value; document section/paragraph when stable; image bounding rectangle; email message ID, sender, date, body/attachment region. If a source cannot provide a stable precise location, the proposal MUST be flagged `LOCATION_IMPRECISE`.

### 10.4 Originality and signatures

The platform records document hashes and observed signature metadata. It MUST NOT claim a document is authentic merely because a hash exists or a signature parses. Signature validation and trust chains require separate explicit capabilities.

## 11. Extraction and validation pipeline

### 11.1 Pipeline

File-type parser extracts structure and text; OCR runs only where required and preserves coordinates; candidate control families are selected deterministically from request/document context; a structured extraction model proposes values, units, methods, and evidence locations; deterministic validators check type, unit, range, enumeration, date, and required context; duplicate and contradiction checks compare current entity claims; proposals enter human review.

### 11.2 Model contract

Each model call MUST record: provider and model identifier; region/processing location where available; prompt and output schema version; input document/derivative hashes; selected control IDs; raw structured output or a reproducibility record; latency, token/usage, cost, error status; whether customer content may be retained or used for provider training (MUST be disabled contractually for production).

### 11.3 Extraction output

```json
{
  "controlKey": "<pack>-<id>",
  "proposedValue": 48,
  "unit": "V",
  "methodContext": "nominal",
  "evidenceLocations": [ { "documentId": "…", "page": 4, "quote": "Nominal voltage: 48 V" } ],
  "confidence": 0.94,
  "warnings": [],
  "requiresHumanReview": true
}
```

### 11.4 Safety constraints

No free-form model response becomes a stored approved value; document text is untrusted input and MUST NOT alter system instructions or tool permissions; retrieval is restricted to the current tenant, entity/request scope, and authorized documents; a proposal with no source location cannot be accepted; high-risk field families MAY require two-person approval; customer-visible language says "extracted proposal," not "verified fact."

### 11.5 Field-family validators

The MVP SHOULD implement deterministic validators per pack for identifiers and contact fields; dates and batch/serial identifiers; numeric quantities with units; enumerated classifications and statuses; and required document/presence fields. Range validation warns; it does not establish correctness.

## 12. Claims, evidence, and conflict

### 12.1 Claim origin

`SUPPLIER_ASSERTION` (external), `INTERNAL_ASSERTION`, `EXTRACTION_ACCEPTED`, `IMPORTED_APPROVED_DATA`, `DERIVED_VALUE` (disabled in MVP unless a separately approved calculation exists).

### 12.2 Revision rule

Claims are immutable revisions. Editing creates a new revision linked by `supersedes_claim_id`. Evidence links and approvals apply to a specific revision.

### 12.3 Conflict detection

A potential conflict is created when active claims for the same entity/control differ after canonical unit conversion, or disagree in qualitative enumeration/document status. The engine MUST preserve all claims; show sources and timestamps; distinguish equivalent unit conversions from material differences; avoid automatically choosing the newest claim; require a reviewer to select/supersede, document a conditional scope, or keep the conflict open.

### 12.4 Staleness

Triggered by explicit evidence expiry; party/entity relationship ending; entity revision after approval; a control snapshot requiring a new format or method; a customer-defined review interval; source document supersession. The original approval remains historically visible.

## 13. Readiness engine

### 13.1 Inputs

Selected `ControlSnapshot`; entity classification and facts; active approved claims; evidence availability/classification; conflicts; staleness rules; pending submissions/proposals; recorded applicability overrides and rationale.

### 13.2 Deterministic algorithm

For each snapshot control: evaluate applicability; if unknown, return `CONDITIONAL`; if excluded by the snapshot, return `NOT_YET_REQUIRED` or `NOT_APPLICABLE` with source explanation; if an unresolved conflict exists, return `CONFLICTING`; if no approved claim exists, return `PENDING_REVIEW` when a pending item exists else `MISSING`; if approved evidence is stale or inaccessible, return `STALE`; if the approved claim meets the evidence-presence and validation contract, return `EVIDENCED`. The engine must be pure/replayable for the same versioned inputs.

### 13.3 Overrides

An authorized technical/compliance user may record an applicability override only with: selected result; rationale; actor and time; referenced specialist/source where applicable; effective entity revision; expiry/review date. Overrides never change the global control snapshot.

### 13.4 Snapshot integrity

A readiness snapshot includes canonical serialized inputs and a cryptographic hash. The hash supports change detection; it is not a regulatory signature or authenticity guarantee.

## 14. Export and interoperability

### 14.1 Canonical export

The canonical JSON export MUST include: organization and responsible-organization identifiers supplied by the customer; entity identifiers/facts; selected control snapshot and source metadata; each control key, official identifier/name, applicability result, readiness state; approved claim value/unit/method and origin; evidence index with document hashes and locations; classification and sharing restrictions; unresolved exceptions; approvals and timestamps; export schema and mapping versions; pack key and version.

### 14.2 Additional formats

CSV matrix; evidence-index CSV; human-readable HTML/PDF summary from the same snapshot; downstream-specific JSON/CSV profiles after validation; ZIP package with manifest and optional source/redacted files.

### 14.3 Export safeguards

Default exports MUST exclude source files and restricted data unless explicitly selected; a preflight summary MUST show access classes and estimated package size; missing required items MAY block a "ready-only" profile but MUST allow an explicit exceptions export; export content MUST be generated only from an immutable readiness snapshot; every exported file MUST appear in the manifest with hash, media type, and classification.

### 14.4 Authority and downstream integrations

Direct integration is `LATER/GATE`. Before enabling production writes, validate: enrollment and authorization model; identifier requirements; target metadata/data model; idempotency and correction behavior; access-class handling; API limits and error semantics; customer approval and rollback paths; standard-support evidence; independent domain and security review.

## 18. Notifications and inbound email

### 18.1 Outbound

Templates are versioned and localized; sensitive values and attachments are excluded; messages link to authenticated/scoped views; delivery, bounce, complaint, and suppression states are stored; operational notices are distinguished from marketing.

### 18.2 Inbound

Use per-request unpredictable addresses or signed routing tokens; authenticate provider webhook signatures; preserve original message ID, sender, recipients, time, subject, normalized body, and attachment hashes; do not trust sender identity from the From header alone; route ambiguous or unauthorized messages to a safe review queue without tenant context.

## 20. Audit and observability

### 20.1 Auditable actions

Authentication/MFA and membership changes; support-access grant/use/revocation; pack installation/activation; entity/control-snapshot creation/adoption; request creation/send/cancel/reopen; token creation/use/revocation; document upload/download/classification/delete; extraction run and proposal decision; claim approval/rejection/supersession; applicability override; readiness snapshot/export/share creation; reviewer access/download; retention/deletion action.

### 20.2 Audit properties

Append-only to application users; corrections are new events. The system SHOULD use a tamper-evident chain or periodic signed digest after the core event model is stable; marketing MUST NOT call the log immutable or tamper-proof until that mechanism is independently verified.

### 20.3 Operational telemetry

Request rate/error/latency by route and tenant-safe dimensions; queue depth and age; upload/scan/parse/extraction/notification/export outcomes; token-use anomalies; authorization denials; storage and provider usage/cost; data-deletion backlog; per-pack catalog-update overdue. Logs MUST use correlation IDs and exclude document text, claim values, access tokens, and signed URLs.

## 21. Retention, backup, and deletion

### 21.1 Retention

Tenant policy controls active evidence, exports, contributor drafts, expired links, and audit retention within contractual minima/maxima. A document with active claim links cannot be casually deleted; the UI must explain impact and offer controlled supersession/redaction/archive. Legal-hold labeling prevents automated purge but does not establish legal validity.

### 21.2 Deletion

A workflow: authorized request and impact preview; optional export; re-authentication and second confirmation for organization deletion; revoke links and stop jobs; tombstone records needed to complete the purge; delete originals, versions, derivatives, exports, indexes, and provider artifacts; record completion evidence without retaining deleted content; disclose backup expiry behavior.

### 21.3 Backup

Encrypted automated database backups with restore tests; object versioning or equivalent recovery bounded by purge policy; restore procedures reestablish tenant and classification controls; quarterly restoration exercise before broad launch.

## 22. Testing and validation

### 22.1 Automated tests

Unit tests for applicability, unit normalization, readiness, conflict, staleness, and state transitions; schema/contract tests for every API and export profile; database isolation tests attempting cross-tenant and cross-pack identifiers for every scoped resource; authorization matrix tests for roles, contributor tokens, reviewer shares, support grants; golden-document extraction tests with licensed/synthetic fixtures and expected locations; prompt-injection and malicious-document tests; file-upload security tests; email spoofing/routing tests; accessibility checks plus manual keyboard/screen-reader acceptance; responsive browser tests; backup restore and deletion completion tests.

### 22.2 Extraction evaluation

Per field family: proposal precision; recall where a target exists; exact source-location accuracy; unit accuracy; wrong-entity association rate; reviewer acceptance/edit/rejection rate; minutes saved versus manual entry. No single blended "AI accuracy" number is sufficient.

### 22.3 Security validation

Threat model before customer uploads; dependency and static analysis in CI; dynamic application tests in staging; external penetration test before self-serve launch; incident tabletop covering a leaked contributor link and a cross-tenant access attempt.

### 22.4 Pack/control validation

Two-person review of every production control snapshot; source URL, publication date, retrieval date, and hash recorded; automated check that the pack's declared control count loads exactly once; classification/applicability sampling against the official source; regression tests for the pack's declared special cases.

## 23. Pre-build feasibility gates

- **FSG-001 — Real document extraction.** Using ≥30 redacted/licensed documents from three organizations: prove exact page/sheet/cell evidence locations; evaluate ≥5 field families; demonstrate wrong-entity association is visible and never auto-approved; record cost and latency; show reviewer time savings.
- **FSG-002 — Contributor link completion.** With ≥10 external participants across phone and desktop: complete without account creation or live training; test saved drafts, expiry, resend, wrong recipient, clarification, decline, receipt; confirm security controls do not make normal use impractical.
- **FSG-003 — Tenancy, pack, and restricted evidence.** Prove database and object isolation with adversarial cross-tenant and cross-pack tests; prove classification exclusions in search, export, logs, notifications, and reviewer views; exercise time-bound support access.
- **FSG-004 — Snapshot and migration.** Seed all controls from the active pack's first snapshot; create a synthetic new snapshot with additions, applicability changes, and renamed formats; show impact without rewriting historical readiness; migrate one entity explicitly and reproduce both results.
- **FSG-005 — Portable export.** Generate canonical JSON, CSV matrix, evidence index, and human summary from one snapshot; re-import canonical JSON into a clean tenant or validation harness without losing lineage; produce at least one downstream mapping agreed with a design partner.
- **FSG-006 — Deletion and incident recovery.** Delete a test organization across all stores and verify completion; restore a separate tenant from backup; revoke all active contributor/reviewer links during a simulated incident.

Failure of FSG-001, FSG-003, or FSG-004 blocks a self-serve MVP. If contributor links need manual support but customers pay for the result, continue only as a transparently concierge-assisted service.

## 25. MVP technical acceptance criteria

1. The active pack's declared controls load exactly once with source and classification applicability.
2. The readiness engine is deterministic and replayable.
3. Historical snapshots remain reproducible after a new control snapshot is added.
4. Original evidence objects and extraction proposals are immutable/versioned.
5. No extraction proposal can become approved without an authorized review decision.
6. Unit-equivalent claims do not generate false conflicts; materially different claims do.
7. Cross-tenant and cross-pack database, object, search, export, and token access tests fail closed.
8. Contributor tokens expire and revoke immediately and cannot enumerate other requests.
9. Malware/quarantined files cannot be viewed or processed by extraction.
10. Export manifests match package contents and classification selections.
11. Every defined auditable action has a successful test.
12. Customer deletion removes content across documented stores within the contractual window.
13. Required responsive and accessibility acceptance scenarios pass.
14. FSG-001 through FSG-006 have recorded results and blockers.
