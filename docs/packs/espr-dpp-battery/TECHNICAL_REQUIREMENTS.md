# Passport Inbox — Technical Requirements

**Document type:** Technical Requirements Document (TRD)  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Proposed / discovery  
**Last updated:** August 30, 2026  
**Working product name:** Passport Inbox (placeholder)

Related documents: [Business Requirements](BUSINESS_REQUIREMENTS.md) and [Detailed Design](detailed-design/README.md)

> This document specifies an evidence-readiness platform. It does not specify an automated legal decision engine, conformity assessment service, public battery-passport host, or production DPP Registry integration.

---

## 1. Purpose

Passport Inbox must convert fragmented supplier and internal documents into a controlled, source-linked, human-approved readiness record. The central technical challenge is preserving the distinctions among:

- a regulatory or guidance source;
- a versioned control interpretation;
- category/model applicability;
- a supplier or internal assertion;
- an automated extraction proposal;
- original evidence and its exact location;
- human approval or rejection;
- current readiness for a named snapshot;
- an export made for a particular downstream purpose.

The platform must remain honest when information is missing, contradictory, stale, conditional, or based on superseded guidance.

## 2. Scope and Constraints

### 2.1 In scope

- Multi-tenant organizations, workspaces, users, roles, and invitations.
- Product and battery-model records for EV, LMT, and industrial categories.
- Versioned regulatory/control snapshots, seeded initially from the Commission's 15 August 2026 guidance.
- Assignment of data points to internal owners and suppliers.
- Supplier requests using expiring, scoped links and inbound email.
- Original-document storage, malware scanning, metadata, and access classification.
- OCR/structured parsing and source-bound extraction proposals.
- Human claim review, conflict resolution, supersession, and approvals.
- Deterministic readiness evaluation and immutable snapshots.
- Portable exports and restricted reviewer sharing.
- Audit, notifications, retention, deletion, observability, and support controls.

### 2.2 Out of scope for MVP

- Legal opinions or automated legal applicability decisions.
- Conformity assessment or certification.
- Public passport publication, QR/data-carrier generation, or resolver hosting.
- Production DPP Registry writes.
- Automatic engineering calculations or test validation.
- Live BMS telemetry and individual-battery lifecycle processing.
- Native iOS/Android apps and browser extensions.
- Cross-tenant supplier-data reuse without explicit, granular supplier authorization.

### 2.3 External constraints

- The Commission guidance is non-authoritative and may change.
- Units and formats for some data points remain subject to later clarification.
- DPP Registry and access-right behavior may evolve before February 2027.
- Documents may contain commercially sensitive composition, test, and sourcing information.
- Suppliers may work in multiple languages and on mobile devices.
- A customer may use a passport publisher with its own schema and validation rules.

## 3. Architecture Principles

1. **Modular monolith first.** Prefer one deployable application plus asynchronous workers until scaling or isolation evidence justifies services.
2. **Canonical domain, adapters at boundaries.** Publisher schemas and extraction providers must not define the internal data model.
3. **Immutable source and history.** Originals, control snapshots, submissions, claim revisions, approvals, and exports are append-oriented.
4. **Human authority over extracted values.** Automation may suggest; only an authorized human can approve.
5. **Tenant isolation by construction.** Every tenant-owned row and object carries a tenant ID; access is enforced in application and database layers.
6. **Least-knowledge supplier links.** A request token exposes only the request and explicitly shared context.
7. **Deterministic readiness.** Readiness is computed from stored rules and states, not generated by an LLM.
8. **Explicit provenance.** Every derived object identifies inputs, actor/process, versions, and time.
9. **Provider portability.** Authentication, object storage, OCR/LLM, email, and export adapters have internal interfaces.
10. **No silent reinterpretation.** New controls create impact records and optional migrations; they never rewrite historical snapshots.

## 4. Recommended MVP Technology Shape

The requirements are implementation-language independent. A pragmatic reference stack is:

The production decision MUST follow the feasibility and data-residency gates in section 23.

## 7. Control Snapshot and Applicability Model

### 7.1 Initial snapshot

The seed snapshot is `EC-BP-2026-08-15`, representing the Commission's version 2.0 guidance with 71 data points. The catalog MUST preserve:

- official data-point number and name;
- cited Batteries Regulation article/annex;
- EV, LMT, and industrial applicability wording;
- whether the guidance labels the point mandatory, optional, conditional, duplicate/not to fill, or not to fill as of February 2027;
- the guidance disclaimer and retrieval metadata.

### 7.2 Applicability output

The engine may return only:

- `REQUIRED_BY_SNAPSHOT`;
- `OPTIONAL_IF_AVAILABLE`;
- `CONDITIONAL_FACT_REQUIRED`;
- `NOT_YET_REQUIRED_BY_SNAPSHOT`;
- `DUPLICATE_SOURCE_FIELD`;
- `NOT_APPLICABLE_TO_CATEGORY`;
- `NEEDS_SPECIALIST_REVIEW`.

The engine MUST NOT return `LEGALLY_COMPLIANT` or equivalent.

### 7.3 Expression model

Applicability expressions MUST be deterministic and inspectable. Example:

```json
{
  "all": [
    { "fact": "battery.category", "in": ["LMT"] },
    { "snapshot": "EC-BP-2026-08-15" }
  ],
  "result": "REQUIRED_BY_SNAPSHOT"
}
```

Unknown facts yield `CONDITIONAL_FACT_REQUIRED`, not a guessed answer.

### 7.4 Snapshot migration

When a new source is approved:

1. Create a new immutable `ControlSnapshot`.
2. Produce `ControlChange` rows against the previous snapshot.
3. Calculate impacted models without changing their selected snapshot.
4. Show customers additions, removals, applicability changes, name/source changes, and format/unit changes.
5. Require an authorized user to adopt the new snapshot per model.
6. Re-evaluate claims; do not discard previously approved evidence.

## 8. Workflow State Machines

### 8.1 Battery model

`DRAFT -> COLLECTING -> REVIEWING -> EVIDENCE_READY`

Alternative states: `BLOCKED`, `OUTDATED_SNAPSHOT`, `ARCHIVED`.

`EVIDENCE_READY` is derived from a readiness snapshot; it is not directly editable.

### 8.2 Evidence request

`DRAFT -> SCHEDULED -> SENT -> OPENED -> IN_PROGRESS -> SUBMITTED -> UNDER_REVIEW -> CLOSED`

Alternative terminal states: `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED`, `CANCELLED`.

Resending MUST not create duplicate request items. A reopened request creates a new submission version.

### 8.3 Document

`UPLOADING -> QUARANTINED -> SCANNING -> AVAILABLE -> PARSING -> PARSED`

Alternative states: `REJECTED_MALWARE`, `UNSUPPORTED`, `PARSE_FAILED`, `DELETED_PENDING_PURGE`, `PURGED`.

No user or extraction worker may download a quarantined or malware-rejected object.

### 8.4 Extraction proposal

`GENERATED -> VALIDATION_WARNING? -> PENDING_REVIEW -> ACCEPTED_AS_CLAIM | REJECTED | SUPERSEDED`

An accepted proposal creates a claim and review decision. The proposal remains immutable.

### 8.5 Claim

`ASSERTED -> PENDING_REVIEW -> APPROVED -> SUPERSEDED`

Alternative states: `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN`. Approved claims may later become `STALE` through evaluation without rewriting approval history.

### 8.6 Export

`QUEUED -> VALIDATING -> GENERATING -> AVAILABLE -> EXPIRED`

Alternative states: `BLOCKED`, `FAILED`, `REVOKED`.

## 10. Document Intake and Evidence Requirements

### 10.1 Supported MVP formats

- PDF, including scanned PDFs.
- PNG, JPEG, and TIFF images.
- XLSX and CSV.
- DOCX and plain text.
- EML or provider-normalized inbound email with attachments.

Password-protected, signed, macro-enabled, archive, CAD, and proprietary laboratory files may be stored only under explicit supported rules; otherwise they are `UNSUPPORTED` with manual guidance.

### 10.2 Ingestion controls

1. Stream upload to quarantine; do not execute active content.
2. Validate media signature rather than trusting extension.
3. Calculate SHA-256 before derivative generation.
4. Run malware and archive-bomb checks.
5. Store the original as immutable/versioned.
6. Extract safe metadata and normalized text in an isolated worker.
7. Create derivatives linked to the original hash and tool version.
8. Require model/supplier associations to be confirmed when not request-derived.

### 10.3 Evidence locations

Locations MUST support:

- PDF page plus bounding rectangle and quoted text;
- spreadsheet sheet, row, column/cell range, and displayed/formula value;
- document section/paragraph when stable;
- image bounding rectangle;
- email message ID, sender, date, and body/attachment region.

If a source cannot provide a stable precise location, the proposal MUST be flagged `LOCATION_IMPRECISE`.

### 10.4 Originality and signatures

The platform records document hashes and observed signature metadata. It MUST NOT claim a document is authentic merely because a hash exists or a PDF signature parses. Signature validation, trust chains, and issuer validation require separate explicit capabilities.

## 11. Extraction and Validation Pipeline

### 11.1 Pipeline

1. File-type-specific parser extracts structure and text.
2. OCR runs only where required and preserves page coordinates.
3. Candidate control families are selected deterministically from request/document context.
4. A structured extraction model proposes values, units, methods, and evidence locations.
5. Deterministic validators check type, unit, range, enumeration, date, and required context.
6. Duplicate and contradiction checks compare current model claims.
7. Proposals enter human review.

### 11.2 Model contract

Each model call MUST record:

- provider and model identifier;
- region or processing location where available;
- prompt and output schema version;
- input document/derivative hashes;
- selected control IDs;
- raw structured output or a securely retained reproducibility record;
- latency, token/usage, cost, and error status;
- whether customer content may be retained or used for provider training (MUST be disabled contractually for production).

### 11.3 Extraction output

```json
{
  "controlKey": "EC-BP-DP-027",
  "proposedValue": 48,
  "unit": "V",
  "methodContext": "nominal",
  "evidenceLocations": [
    { "documentId": "...", "page": 4, "quote": "Nominal voltage: 48 V" }
  ],
  "confidence": 0.94,
  "warnings": [],
  "requiresHumanReview": true
}
```

### 11.4 Safety constraints

- No free-form model response becomes a stored approved value.
- Document text is untrusted input and MUST NOT alter system instructions or tool permissions.
- Retrieval is restricted to the current tenant, model/request scope, and authorized documents.
- A proposal with no source location is not reviewable and cannot be accepted.
- High-risk field families MAY require two-person approval.
- Customer-visible language describes “extracted proposal,” not “verified fact.”

### 11.5 Field-family validators

The MVP SHOULD implement deterministic validators for:

- identifiers and organization contact fields;
- dates and model/batch/serial identifiers;
- mass, capacity, voltage, power, resistance, efficiency, and percentages;
- enumerated battery category, chemistry, and lifecycle status;
- required document/presence fields such as declaration, test report, dismantling information, and safety measures.

Range validation warns; it does not establish scientific correctness.

## 12. Claims, Evidence, and Conflict Requirements

### 12.1 Claim origin

Every claim has one origin:

- `SUPPLIER_ASSERTION`;
- `INTERNAL_ASSERTION`;
- `EXTRACTION_ACCEPTED`;
- `IMPORTED_APPROVED_DATA`;
- `DERIVED_VALUE` (disabled in MVP unless a separately approved calculation exists).

### 12.2 Revision rule

Claims are immutable revisions. Editing creates a new revision linked by `supersedes_claim_id`. Evidence links and approvals apply to a specific revision.

### 12.3 Conflict detection

A potential conflict is created when active claims for the same model/control differ after canonical unit conversion or disagree in qualitative enumeration/document status. The engine MUST:

- preserve all claims;
- show sources and timestamps;
- distinguish equivalent unit conversions from material differences;
- avoid automatically choosing the newest claim;
- require a reviewer to select/supersede, document a conditional scope, or keep the conflict open.

### 12.4 Staleness

Staleness may be triggered by:

- explicit evidence expiry;
- supplier/model relationship ending;
- model revision after evidence approval;
- control snapshot requiring a new format or method;
- customer-defined review interval;
- source document supersession.

The original approval remains historically visible.

## 13. Readiness Engine

### 13.1 Inputs

- selected `ControlSnapshot`;
- battery category and model facts;
- active approved claims;
- evidence availability/classification;
- conflicts;
- staleness rules;
- pending submissions/proposals;
- recorded applicability overrides and rationale.

### 13.2 Deterministic algorithm

For each snapshot control:

1. Evaluate applicability expression.
2. If unknown, return `CONDITIONAL`.
3. If excluded by the snapshot, return `NOT_YET_REQUIRED` or `NOT_APPLICABLE` with source explanation.
4. If an unresolved conflict exists, return `CONFLICTING`.
5. If no approved claim exists, return `PENDING_REVIEW` when a pending item exists, otherwise `MISSING`.
6. If approved evidence is stale or inaccessible, return `STALE`.
7. If the approved claim meets the evidence-presence and validation contract, return `EVIDENCED`.

The engine must be pure/replayable for the same versioned inputs.

### 13.3 Overrides

An authorized technical/compliance user may record an applicability override only with:

- selected result;
- rationale;
- actor and time;
- referenced specialist/source where applicable;
- effective model revision;
- expiry/review date.

Overrides never change the global control snapshot.

### 13.4 Snapshot integrity

A readiness snapshot includes canonical serialized inputs and a cryptographic hash. The hash supports change detection; it is not a regulatory signature or authenticity guarantee.

## 14. Export and Interoperability Requirements

### 14.1 Canonical export

The canonical JSON export MUST include:

- organization and responsible-operator identifiers supplied by the customer;
- product and model identifiers/facts;
- selected control snapshot and source metadata;
- each control key, official number/name, applicability result, readiness state;
- approved claim value/unit/method and origin;
- evidence index with document hashes and locations;
- classification and sharing restrictions;
- unresolved exceptions;
- approvals and timestamps;
- export schema and mapping versions.

### 14.2 Additional formats

- CSV matrix for operations.
- Evidence-index CSV.
- Human-readable HTML/PDF summary generated from the same snapshot.
- Publisher-specific JSON/CSV profiles after validation.
- ZIP package with manifest and optional source/redacted files.

### 14.3 Export safeguards

- Default exports MUST exclude source files and restricted data unless explicitly selected by an authorized user.
- A preflight summary MUST show access classes and estimated package size.
- Missing required items MAY block a “ready-only” profile but MUST allow an explicit exceptions export.
- Export content MUST be generated only from an immutable readiness snapshot.
- Every exported file MUST appear in the manifest with hash, media type, and classification.

### 14.4 DPP Registry and publisher integrations

Direct integration is `LATER/GATE`. Before enabling production writes, validate:

- organization enrollment and authorization model;
- identifier requirements;
- registry metadata/data model;
- idempotency and correction behavior;
- access-class handling;
- API limits and error semantics;
- customer approval and rollback/correction paths;
- evidence that six published and remaining standards are supported as applicable;
- independent domain and security review.

- Collect professional/contact data only as necessary for workflow.

- Separate organization evidence from platform analytics.
- Provide role-appropriate privacy notices and processor/subprocessor disclosures.
- Support data-subject and customer-contract workflows before launch in the selected jurisdiction.
- Do not use customer or supplier content for generalized model training without a separate explicit opt-in and legal basis; default is prohibited.

- Versioned REST or equivalent resource API under `/api/v1`.

### 17.3 Webhooks

MVP webhooks MAY include export completed, request submitted, and model readiness changed. They MUST be signed, retried with backoff, idempotent, tenant-scoped, and redact restricted evidence.

## 18. Notifications and Inbound Email

### 18.1 Outbound

- Templates are versioned and localized.
- Sensitive values and attachments are excluded; messages link to authenticated/scoped views.
- Delivery, bounce, complaint, and unsubscribe/suppression states are stored.
- Operational notices required for active requests are distinguished from marketing.

### 18.2 Inbound

- Use per-request unpredictable addresses or signed routing tokens.
- Authenticate provider webhook signatures.
- Preserve original message ID, sender, recipients, time, subject, normalized body, and attachment hashes.
- Do not trust sender identity solely because the From header matches a supplier contact.
- Route ambiguous or unauthorized messages to a safe review queue without exposing tenant context.

## 20. Audit and Observability

### 20.1 Auditable actions

At minimum:

- authentication/MFA and membership changes;
- support-access grant/use/revocation;
- model/control snapshot creation/adoption;
- request creation/send/cancel/reopen;
- token creation/use/revocation;
- document upload/download/classification/delete;
- extraction run and proposal decision;
- claim approval/rejection/supersession;
- applicability override;
- readiness snapshot/export/share creation;
- reviewer access/download;
- retention/deletion action.

### 20.2 Audit properties

Audit events are append-only to application users. Corrections are new events. The system SHOULD use a tamper-evident chain or periodic signed digest after the core event model is stable; marketing MUST not call the log immutable or tamper-proof until that mechanism is independently verified.

### 20.3 Operational telemetry

- Request rate/error/latency by route and tenant-safe dimensions.
- Queue depth and age.
- Upload, scan, parse, extraction, notification, and export outcomes.
- Token use anomalies.
- Authorization denials.
- Storage and AI/provider usage/cost.
- Data-deletion backlog.

Logs MUST use correlation IDs and exclude document text, claim values, access tokens, and signed URLs.

## 21. Retention, Backup, and Deletion

### 21.1 Retention

- Tenant policy controls active evidence, exports, supplier drafts, expired links, and audit retention within contractual minima/maxima.
- A document with active claim links cannot be casually deleted; the UI must explain impact and offer controlled supersession/redaction/archive paths.
- Legal-hold labeling prevents automated purge but does not itself establish legal validity.

### 21.2 Deletion

Deletion is a workflow:

1. Authorized request and impact preview.
2. Optional export.
3. Re-authentication and second confirmation for organization deletion.
4. Revoke links and stop jobs.
5. Tombstone database records needed to complete the purge safely.
6. Delete originals, versions, derivatives, exports, indexes, and provider artifacts.
7. Record completion evidence without retaining deleted content.
8. Disclose backup expiry behavior.

### 21.3 Backup

- Encrypted automated database backups with restore tests.
- Object versioning or equivalent recovery for accidental deletion, bounded by purge policy.
- Restore procedures must reestablish tenant and classification controls.
- Quarterly restoration exercise before broad launch.

## 22. Testing and Validation Strategy

### 22.1 Automated tests

- Unit tests for applicability, unit normalization, readiness, conflict, staleness, and state transitions.
- Schema/contract tests for every API and export profile.
- Database isolation tests that attempt cross-tenant identifiers for every tenant resource.
- Authorization matrix tests for roles, supplier tokens, reviewer shares, and support grants.
- Golden-document extraction tests with licensed/synthetic fixtures and expected locations.
- Prompt-injection and malicious-document tests.
- File-upload security tests including mismatched signatures, oversized files, archives, malware fixture, and parser failure.
- Email spoofing/routing tests.
- Accessibility checks plus manual keyboard/screen-reader acceptance.
- Responsive browser tests for required form factors.
- Backup restore and deletion completion tests.

### 22.2 Extraction evaluation

Measure per field family:

- proposal precision;
- proposal recall where a target exists;
- exact source-location accuracy;
- unit accuracy;
- wrong-model association rate;
- reviewer acceptance/edit/rejection rate;
- minutes saved versus manual entry.

No single blended “AI accuracy” number is sufficient.

### 22.3 Security validation

- Threat model before customer uploads.
- Dependency and static analysis in CI.
- Dynamic application tests in staging.
- External penetration test before self-serve launch.
- Incident tabletop covering a leaked supplier link and cross-tenant access attempt.

### 22.4 Regulatory/control validation

- Two-person review of every production control snapshot.
- Source URL, publication date, retrieval date, and hash recorded.
- Automated check that all 71 seed data points are present exactly once.
- Category/applicability sampling against the official guidance.
- Regression tests for documented special cases such as data points 16, 17–23, 25, 31–39, 44, and 51–71.

## 23. Pre-Build Feasibility Gates

### FSG-001 — Real document extraction

Using at least 30 redacted or licensed documents from three organizations:

- prove exact page/sheet/cell evidence locations;
- evaluate at least five field families;
- demonstrate that wrong-model association is visible and never auto-approved;
- record cost and latency;
- show reviewer time savings.

### FSG-002 — Supplier link completion

With at least 10 external participants across phone and desktop:

- complete without account creation or live training;
- test saved drafts, expiry, resend, wrong recipient, clarification, decline, and receipt;
- confirm security controls do not make normal use impractical.

### FSG-003 — Tenancy and restricted evidence

- Prove database and object isolation with adversarial cross-tenant tests.
- Prove classification exclusions in search, export, logs, notifications, and reviewer views.
- Exercise time-bound support access.

### FSG-004 — Snapshot and migration

- Seed all 71 controls from `EC-BP-2026-08-15`.
- Create a synthetic new snapshot with additions, applicability changes, and renamed formats.
- Show impact without rewriting historical readiness.
- Migrate one model explicitly and reproduce both results.

### FSG-005 — Portable export

- Generate canonical JSON, CSV matrix, evidence index, and human summary from one snapshot.
- Re-import canonical JSON into a clean tenant or validation harness without losing lineage.
- Produce at least one downstream mapping agreed with a design partner.

### FSG-006 — Deletion and incident recovery

- Delete a test organization across all stores and verify completion.
- Restore a separate tenant from backup.
- Revoke all active supplier/reviewer links during a simulated incident.

Failure of FSG-001, FSG-003, or FSG-004 blocks a self-serve MVP. If supplier links need manual support but customers pay for the result, continue only as a transparently concierge-assisted service.

## 25. MVP Technical Acceptance Criteria

1. All 71 seed controls load exactly once with source and category applicability.
2. The readiness engine is deterministic and replayable.
3. Historical snapshots remain reproducible after a new control snapshot is added.
4. Original evidence objects and extraction proposals are immutable/versioned.
5. No extraction proposal can become approved without an authorized review decision.
6. Unit-equivalent claims do not generate false conflicts; materially different claims do.
7. Cross-tenant database, object, search, export, and token access tests fail closed.
8. Supplier tokens expire and revoke immediately and cannot enumerate other requests.
9. Malware/quarantined files cannot be viewed or processed by extraction.
10. Export manifests match package contents and classification selections.
11. Every defined auditable action has a successful test.
12. Customer deletion removes content across documented stores within the contractual window.
13. Required responsive and accessibility acceptance scenarios pass.
14. FSG-001 through FSG-006 have recorded results and blockers.
