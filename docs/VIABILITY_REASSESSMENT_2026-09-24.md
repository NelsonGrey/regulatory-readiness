# Product viability reassessment — September 24, 2026

**Document type:** Discovery checkpoint

**Status:** Current recommendation; commercial validation still required

**Assessment date:** September 24, 2026

**Decision:** Continue the product, narrow the entry market, and validate a
partner-led offer before production hardening.

This checkpoint records a reassessment of the product thesis against the
repository's current implementation, regulatory developments, standards changes,
and the 2026 competitive landscape. It is a product decision record, not legal
advice, a conformity assessment, or evidence that the market has been validated.

## 1. Executive conclusion

The underlying problem remains credible: organizations still need to determine
which requirements apply, collect information from internal and external parties,
preserve source provenance, reconcile conflicting assertions, and produce dated,
reviewable evidence packages.

The original go-to-market thesis has weakened, however:

- EAA accessibility software now includes capable, affordable platforms combining
  automated and manual testing, remediation workflows, statements, monitoring,
  and audit evidence.
- Broad multi-framework evidence management is offered by established GRC vendors
  and a growing group of lower-cost EU-focused products.
- EU simplification has delayed or narrowed some candidate obligations, reducing
  the near-term SME opportunity for EUDR, CSRD, and CSDDD packs.
- Battery-passport demand is now urgent, but the category contains numerous
  specialist and enterprise vendors with supplier collection, identifiers, QR
  publication, and passport hosting.

The engine remains useful and differentiated in implementation quality, especially
its source-bound assertions, explicit conflicts, scoped contributor links,
immutable snapshots, and portable exports. Those capabilities are not sufficient
on their own to support a broad self-serve category claim.

## 2. What has changed

### 2.1 Regulatory demand is real, not hypothetical

- The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 28, 2025.
- Cyber Resilience Act reporting obligations applied from September 11, 2026;
  the main product obligations apply from December 11, 2027.
- The battery-passport requirement remains scheduled for February 18, 2027, and
  the Commission published updated category-by-category data-point guidance in
  August 2026.

These developments support the general evidence-readiness problem. They do not,
by themselves, establish willingness to pay for this product.

### 2.2 EAA is no longer a soft competitive entry layer

The earlier sequencing assumption described EAA as a large, live market with a
soft mid-bottom competitive layer. By September 2026, accessibility platforms
advertise combinations of full EN 301 549 audit coverage, automated scanning,
guided and manual testing, remediation tracking, accessibility-statement
generation, immutable audits, and monitoring at prices accessible to smaller
organizations.

The current EAA pack remains a draft discovery artifact. It contains 20 controls,
an unverified source checksum, no completed two-person review, and no claim of
complete EN 301 549 coverage. It demonstrates the engine; it is not a sellable
assurance catalog.

### 2.3 The accessibility baseline is moving

EN 301 549 v4.1.1 was published in September 2026. It incorporates WCAG 2.2 and
adds mapping intended to support the EAA. It was not yet cited in the Official
Journal at the assessment date, so it was not yet the harmonized legal reference.

This creates both:

- an opportunity for the engine's immutable versioning and change-impact model;
- an immediate requirement to support an explicit current-versus-future baseline
  without presenting an unpublished or unharmonized standard as binding law.

### 2.4 Several adjacent pack assumptions moved

- EUDR application was postponed to December 30, 2026, with additional time for
  micro and small operators.
- CSRD and CSDDD were materially narrowed and delayed through the EU simplification
  program, reducing their immediate relevance to the initial SME segment.
- CRA is commercially timely, but its live incident-reporting workflow is outside
  the engine's stated boundary of non-transactional evidence preparation. A CRA
  pack must remain focused on documentation readiness unless that product boundary
  is deliberately changed.
- Battery passports retain a hard near-term deadline, but a crowded vendor field
  makes a generic passport publisher an unattractive entry position.

## 3. Revised viable position

The strongest current hypothesis is a **partner-facing evidence operations
system** for accessibility consultancies, regulatory advisers, testing firms, or
specialist agencies that manage recurring work across multiple clients.

The product should complement scanners, audit tools, laboratories, calculation
engines, and passport publishers. Its job is to:

- request and collect evidence from clients, suppliers, and reviewers without
  requiring every contributor to hold an account;
- preserve originals, assertions, review decisions, conflicts, and exact source
  locations as different objects;
- show missing, conflicting, stale, conditional, and unreviewed items without
  reducing them to a misleading score;
- create immutable, portable client-controlled snapshots; and
- identify which clients and records are affected when a governed control pack
  changes.

For the first validation, EAA remains useful as a demonstration domain, but the
proposed buyer changes from a single small retailer seeking an accessibility tool
to an accessibility consultancy or agency coordinating evidence across several
clients. This avoids competing head-on with scanners while exercising the
engine's strongest workflows.

## 4. Commercial and delivery decision

Do not treat the public self-serve funnel, broad pack library, or EUR 99/299 plan
hypotheses as validated. Do not incur production-hardening costs solely because
the implementation is technically advanced.

Run one narrow paid validation offer:

> Evidence workflow and client dossier infrastructure for accessibility
> consultancies managing recurring EAA / EN 301 549 work.

The engagement should use a real client matter and compare the product with the
partner's current spreadsheets, folders, audit platform, and follow-up process.
The partner remains responsible for technical and legal judgments.

Proceed to production hardening only after the existing per-pack discovery gate
is satisfied:

- at least 15 interviews with in-scope organizations;
- at least five current-workflow demonstrations;
- at least three redacted real document sets;
- at least three paid pilots from independent organizations; and
- at least two customers or partners willing to continue.

Apply the existing stop/redirect criteria without relaxing them because the
software is already built.

## 5. Immediate documentation and product consequences

1. Treat the EAA and battery packs as draft engine demonstrations until their
   source sets, coverage, test vectors, and two-person reviews are complete.
2. Add an EN 301 549 v4.1.1 watch item and preserve the distinction between
   publication and Official Journal harmonization.
3. Replace “EAA funds the engine” with a discovery hypothesis, not a roadmap fact.
4. Keep CSRD and CSDDD on hold; revise EUDR timing before any pack work.
5. Do not add a transactional CRA incident-reporting promise under the current
   product boundary.
6. Validate the partner-led proposition before wiring paid production vendors,
   completing cloud infrastructure, or claiming the concept is commercially
   validated.
